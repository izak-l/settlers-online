# 016 — Fog and scenario 3 (Misty Passage)

## Goal

Some hexes start face down and are revealed when a road or ship reaches them, so the map is explored as you play. Scenario 3 in the rulebook (pages 8–9; our name "Misty Passage") is built on this. Part of M10 (M10e), on the `seafaring` module (spec 015).

## Scope / non-scope

**Scope**

- Fog hexes on maps with the `seafaring` module, revealed one at a time, drawn at the moment of discovery with the game RNG.
- Scenario 3: 3p and 4p fixed maps (with spec 017, variable maps too), 12 VP, no island bonus, starting settlements on one or two islands.

**Non-scope**

- Fog without seafaring. Fog in other scenarios (the format allows it; no scenario 1–8 needs it).

## Rules (rulebook page 8)

- A fog hex shows nothing until it is revealed: no terrain, no token.
- When you place a road or ship (including moving a ship) on an edge with an end at a corner of a fog hex, that hex is revealed: draw the top hex of the scenario's face-down stack.
- A revealed land hex gets a random number disc from the face-down disc stack, and you take 1 card of the resource it produces (every non-sea hex in the stack produces; a desert, if a custom map puts one there, gives no disc and no card).
- A revealed sea hex gets no disc and gives nothing.
- Once revealed, a hex behaves exactly like any other hex of its terrain. A revealed gold hex therefore gives 1 card of your choice as the discovery reward, and later produces like gold.
- If one placement touches two fog hexes, both are revealed, in hex-key order.
- Until revealed, a fog hex counts as sea for placement: ships may use its edges, roads and settlements may not. Once revealed it is whatever it turned out to be.
- Revealed land joins the region of the land it touches, or forms a new region.

## Engine changes

- Fog hexes stay `terrain: "fog"` in `state.map`. Seafaring's `setup` moves the scenario's fog stacks (`scenario.fog.terrain`: counts per terrain; `scenario.fog.tokens`: a list) into hidden zones `fog-terrain` and `fog-tokens`. `viewFor` already reduces hidden zones to a count, so the answer never reaches a client.
- `onPlace` (decision record 0001): after a road or ship lands with an end on a fog hex's corner, draw from the hidden zones (sorted with `cardList`, picked with the game RNG, like the dev deck), write terrain and token into `draft.map.hexes`, emit a public `REVEALED` event, give the reward (a `GOLD_CHOICE` for gold), and recompute regions.
- The graph covers every hex, so a reveal changes no topology; land helpers read the map and see the new terrain at once.
- Tests: reveal on road, ship, ship move and setup placements; two at once; stacks shrink; sea gets no disc and no reward; gold reward; bank short of the reward card; fog counts as sea before the reveal; regions after a reveal; redaction (no player or spectator view contains the stacks before a reveal); replay reproduces the same reveals.

## Server/UI changes

- `packages/maps`: `fog` terrain allowed only with the `seafaring` module; the validator checks the fog stacks match the number of fog hexes, and that discs match the land in the stack. Scenario 3's maps, checked against the rulebook's face-up and face-down component counts.
- `apps/web`: fog hexes drawn as a misty face-down tile; a reveal animation and sound; the log line "P2 discovered pasture (9)".
- `tools/sim`: invariant that the fog stacks plus revealed hexes always add up to the map's original fog count.

## Test plan

- Engine tests above; redaction and replay invariants on scenario 3.
- Maps: scenario 3 validates; mismatched fog stacks are rejected.
- `pnpm sim --games 2000 --seed 1 --scenario <3>` runs clean.
- Manual: explore in the sandbox and online; a spectator sees reveals only as they happen.

## Status

Shipped (M10e, on `m10`), with scenario 3's fixed and variable maps. As built: the scenario block carries `fog: { terrain, tokens }` and `discoveryReward`; seafaring's `canPlace` keeps roads, settlements and the robber off fog-only land; reveals run from `onPlace`, which base now runs after a placement step is settled (so a gold pick for a discovered gold hex sits on top); the seafaring invariant checks the face-down stack always equals the hexes still face down. Revealed land joins the region of land next to it; two regions joined by a reveal are not merged.
